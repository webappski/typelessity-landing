<strong>Before an EU business puts an AI booking widget on its site, it should check five things: where the conversation is processed and stored, including by the AI provider; whether that provider trains on it and how long it keeps it; how consent is asked and withdrawn; how long conversations are kept and how they are erased; and which sub-processors the DPA names. Below are the questions, and Typelessity's answers as the code and the DPA state them — including what we do not offer.</strong>

You, the business, are the controller of your visitors' data; Typelessity is your processor under a data processing agreement (GDPR Article 28). A compliance status is therefore not something a vendor can hand you. What a vendor can give you is a data path you can inspect and a contract that matches it. This article is not legal advice; for anything high-stakes, ask your data protection officer or lawyer.

## What should an EU business check before adding an AI booking widget?

1. **Where the data goes.** Where the service and the database run, and where the AI provider processes the text — which is often a different country. Transfers outside the EEA need a mechanism such as the Standard Contractual Clauses (Articles 44–46).
2. **Training and provider retention.** Whether the AI provider trains on the conversation, and how long it keeps a copy.
3. **Consent.** Whether the visitor is asked before any AI processing starts, and how they withdraw (Article 7).
4. **Retention and erasure.** How long conversations stay, what deletes them, and how an erasure request is handled (Articles 5(1)(e) and 17).
5. **Sub-processors.** A named list in the DPA, with notice before it changes (Article 28).

**Bottom line:** the AI is not the regulated object — the data path is. Ask for each answer from the contract or the code.

## Where does Typelessity process and store data?

The service runs on Vercel in Frankfurt (fra1). The database is Supabase Postgres on AWS in Ireland (eu-west-1). This is the same for every plan; there is no separate residency tier.

Two providers process outside the EEA: OpenAI, which understands the conversation and transcribes voice, and Resend, which delivers request emails to the business. Both process in the United States under the 2021 Standard Contractual Clauses.

Typelessity is a hosted service only. There is no on-premise or self-hosted build, and conversations always go to OpenAI through our account — there is no option to bring your own key or another AI provider.

Every connection the service receives and makes uses TLS, and a booking endpoint must be an https:// URL. Integration secrets you store, such as an API key for your booking system, carry an extra application-level AES-256-GCM encryption.

## Does OpenAI train on the conversations, and how long does it keep them?

OpenAI does not use data sent to its API for training unless the account opts in, and ours has not. The Typelessity DPA also forbids us from using conversation content to train any model, ours or anyone else's.

OpenAI keeps conversation text for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then deletes it. Its transcription endpoint keeps no such copy, and Typelessity never saves voice audio — it exists only while it is being transcribed. We hold no Zero Data Retention approval from OpenAI and cannot shorten its 30 days; if a contract requires Zero Data Retention, we can apply for it, but the decision is OpenAI's.

## How does consent work in the widget?

Before the assistant does anything — typing or speaking — the widget asks the visitor for consent, the same way in every region. The server checks for a consent record before a conversation can start and again on every message. The consent is recorded on our side and remembered in the visitor's browser so they are not asked again, and it can be withdrawn at any time from the privacy panel inside the widget.

The widget has no analytics and sets no cookies. Which legal basis applies to your booking process is your decision as the controller.

## How long is data kept, and how is it erased?

An abandoned conversation — no booking and no activity for 24 hours — is removed by a job that runs once a night, so it is gone within 48 hours of the visitor's last message. A conversation that led to a booking or a forwarded request has no automatic expiry: it is kept until the business erases it. The record that the visitor consented is kept for 3 years and then deleted by the same nightly job.

To erase a conversation, send a written instruction to info@webappski.com naming it. We erase the conversation and its messages and strip the personal content from its delivery record, leaving only status and timing as proof of delivery. Requests covering more than one conversation are handled within 10 working days. There is no self-service erasure or export endpoint.

## Who are the sub-processors?

Appendix B of the [Typelessity DPA](https://webappski.com/en/legal/dpa-typelessity) lists them, each with its contracting entity, where the data is processed and the transfer mechanism:

- **Vercel** — hosting, EU (Frankfurt).
- **Supabase** — database, EU (AWS Ireland).
- **OpenAI** — conversation understanding and speech-to-text, processed in the United States.
- **Resend** — delivery of request emails, United States.

Changes are notified at least 30 days in advance.

## Can the widget be used for health data?

No. The Typelessity DPA does not allow special-category data under GDPR Article 9 — health details must not be collected through the widget. Medical example configurations are demos, not for real patients. US healthcare with HIPAA requirements is not supported either.

## What Typelessity does not offer

So nothing here reads as more than it is:

- no on-premise or self-hosted build;
- no Zero Data Retention with OpenAI;
- no uptime commitment in the standard terms;
- no self-service erasure or export endpoint, and no audit-log product;
- no special-category (health) data.
